🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

Let's work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer. Networking is just three questions, always in order:

1
Where am I? ip addr gives your address (e.g. 172.20.10.2/28 = 14 usable). link/ether is your MAC (local name-tag). If no address → fix DHCP/cable/Wi-Fi first. Never blame the internet yet.
2
How do I leave? ip route shows default via 172.20.10.1 — your exit door. ping the door (expect ~2 ms). Then ping 8.8.8.8 (expect ~200+ ms). If door answers but internet doesn't → routing/filtering, not your PC.
3
Who is who? dig google.com turns names→IPs. ip neigh turns IPs→MACs locally. ss -tln shows what you expose. Port 443 usually means HTTPS, but verify — don't trust the number alone.

Then security: normal first, suspicious second. One odd packet proves nothing. Look for patterns: many ports in seconds (scan), gateway MAC flips (ARP), new DNS server (DHCP), 16× traffic jump (DoS). Correlate logs + packets, then act. That is the whole job.

You will know more than most interview candidates if you can explain: private vs public IP, /24 vs /26, TCP handshake (SYN→SYN/ACK→ACK), DNS→TCP→TLS→HTTP journey, and why encryption hides content but not timing/size.
